# CineTale v1.10.42 — Story Intelligence & Cinematic Direction RC

## Release intent

This release moves CineTale's intelligence upstream so video generation follows a stronger story rather than treating scenes as generic camera coverage. It preserves the validated v1.10.41 automatic scene-edit layer and all existing durable-media contracts.

## What changed

- Added a story-architecture layer inside the world bible: dramatic spine, protagonist goal, stakes, central conflict, theme, midpoint turn, climax, resolution, and open threads.
- Added per-scene dramatic metadata: dramatic purpose, character objective, obstacle/resistance, new information/change, emotional turn, entry state, exit state, handoff, story question, continuity locks, visual progression, and score intent.
- Added a story-quality/cinematic repair pass for live story generation when the initial plan is structurally usable but still weak or repetitive.
- Strengthened story generation rules so every scene must change plot, knowledge, relationship, stakes, emotion, or setup/payoff; redundant scenes are explicitly disallowed.
- Strengthened Story-to-Cinema planning: `visualProgression` is a sequence of distinct filmable actions/reveals/reactions rather than camera-size labels.
- Coverage planning now inherits story beats and scene dramatic purpose. Non-speaking shots are assigned distinct story progression beats instead of being generic alternate takes.
- Speaking-shot direction now inherits scene objective and emotional turn while preserving exact dialogue ownership.
- Veo prompting now includes dramatic spine, scene purpose, objective, obstacle, entry/exit states, new information, emotional turn, assigned story beat, and continuity locks.
- Storyboard art prompting now includes scene dramatic purpose, objective, reveal/change, emotional turn, visual progression, and continuity locks.
- Next-episode generation uses the same story-intelligence contract so future episodes do not fall back to older generic scene planning.
- Existing durable video production contract (`v1.9.86-character-shot-contract`) was deliberately left unchanged so READY paid assets are not invalidated or regenerated merely because story intelligence improved.
- Existing automatic scene editor revision (`v1.10.41-auto-editorial-flow`) and existing shot-timeline migration marker remain unchanged because their persisted contracts did not change.

## Live-provider / browser limitation

This release was not validated with fresh billable Gemini/Veo, ElevenLabs, or lip-sync provider calls during packaging QA. The automated suite validates request construction, state contracts, media safety, and production logic. Final assessment of how strongly a live video model obeys the richer story-direction prompt still requires deployed real-provider testing. No claim of live-provider behavior is made here.

## Recommended first deployed validation

Create a fresh short/episode from a minimal idea and inspect the generated story/scene plan before spending video credits. Confirm that scenes have distinct dramatic changes and the shot plan advances through different actions/reveals rather than repeating one action with different crops. Then generate one scene only and compare continuity and story progression against v1.10.41 behavior.
